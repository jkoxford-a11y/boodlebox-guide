Welcome to the Psychology AI Integration Pilot

A quick read before your first class — what this pilot is, what's different about how you'll use AI in this course, and how to get set up so day one isn't spent on logistics.

What this pilot is

Psychology students are heading into work and graduate study where AI-assisted tools are becoming common — but knowing how to use a chatbot doesn't automatically teach you how to evaluate one. This pilot builds that evaluation skill directly into your psychology coursework: not just using AI, but judging its output the way psychology already trains you to judge evidence — checking assumptions, spotting bias, weighing what a claim is actually based on.

This is a first-year pilot at Columbia College of Missouri, running Fall 2026, in psychology courses with psych majors enrolled. It's an early effort to try this out and gather evidence — not a finished program with proven results yet, and not every psychology course will use it in exactly the same way.

What's different about this class

  • Some coursework is AI-assisted. You'll be encouraged to use AI tools — mainly BoodleBox — for certain kinds of learning and practice, with guidance on how to do that well.
  • Some assessment stays AI-free, on purpose. The parts of your coursework meant to show what you actually know are designed to be completed without AI — not as a "gotcha," but because that's the only way graded work can tell you and your instructor whether the learning actually happened. Your instructor will be clear about which is which for any given assignment.
  • You may be asked to complete a couple of short surveys about your AI knowledge and attitudes, near the start and end of the semester, as part of measuring whether the pilot is working. Participation details and consent will come from your instructor directly — this page isn't the consent form.

The goal isn't to police AI use or to make you avoid it — it's to make sure you leave this course able to use AI and defend your own thinking, not one instead of the other.

Get set up with BoodleBox before day one

BoodleBox is the AI workspace the college provides for this — one account, several AI models, and privacy protections a personal ChatGPT or Claude account doesn't have. Getting your account activated and building one practice study bot before class starts means the first day can go straight to coursework instead of a live demo.

Questions before classes start

  • Is this required? The AI-assisted parts of coursework are part of the class like any other assignment. The optional surveys are separate and consent-based — you'll get real consent information from your instructor, not just this page.
  • Does this affect my grade? Ask your instructor how AI-assisted work factors into your specific course — it varies by class and assignment, and your syllabus is the actual authority on that, not this site.
  • What if I'd rather not use AI at all? Talk to your instructor. The protected-assessment parts of the course are designed to work the same whether or not you use AI to prepare.
  • What happens to my data? BoodleBox is provided through the college with institutional privacy protections. Never put real client, patient, or research-participant data into it or any AI tool — course materials and your own notes are fine.
  • I don't have a BoodleBox account yet. See the setup guide linked above, or ask your instructor or campus IT for your institutional sign-in link.

Have a question this page doesn't answer? Ask your instructor — they're your best first stop, before and after the semester starts.
