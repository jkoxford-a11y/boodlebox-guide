Getting Started with BoodleBox

A student's guide to what BoodleBox is, how to build a study bot, and how to use it without letting it think for you.

What BoodleBox is

BoodleBox is a web-based AI workspace your college provides so you can use several major AI models — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, and others — through one account, instead of juggling separate personal subscriptions. It's built for education, with institutional data-privacy protections that a personal AI account doesn't have — which is why it's the default place to put real course materials.

It also lets you build reusable custom bots: a saved chat, pointed at specific documents, with instructions that make it behave consistently every time you return to it.

BoodleBox is where you're free to experiment and lean on AI heavily while you're learning — because the graded work that actually shows what you know is designed separately, in ways AI can't do for you.

First-time setup

  1. Sign in with your college account at your institution's BoodleBox link (ask your instructor or campus IT if you don't have it).
  2. Take the platform's own interactive getting-started tour if one is offered on first login.
  3. Note the two icons you'll use constantly: the brain icon 🧠 (your Knowledge Bank, where you store files) and the paperclip icon 📎 (attaches files to a specific chat).

Build your first study bot

  1. Save your course materials (lecture notes, textbook chapters, syllabus, rubrics) as PDF, DOCX, or TXT.
  2. Upload them to your Knowledge Bank (🧠 → + Upload Files).
  3. Start a new chat, attach the relevant files (📎 → Attach), and pick a general-purpose model.
  4. Give the bot a clear role, e.g. "You are my study assistant for [course]. Use the attached materials to help me review concepts, quiz me, and explain difficult topics in simple terms."
  5. Switch it to Bot Mode (⋯ menu) so it's saved as a reusable tool, not a one-off chat.
  6. Turn on Knowledge to Context Mode so it reasons over your full documents, not just snippets.
  7. Rename and organize it (e.g. "PSYC 101 Study Bot") so you can find it again.

What to actually ask it

  • "Quiz me on this week's vocabulary."
  • "Summarize the key themes from this week's lectures."
  • "Create a practice test based on the rubric."
  • "Explain [concept] in simpler terms, then check whether I actually understood it by asking me to explain it back."

Two features worth knowing early

Model comparison — you can put the same question to two different models and compare the answers side by side. This isn't just a novelty: models genuinely differ in reasoning quality, and free tools you might use outside BoodleBox may be meaningfully weaker than what your institutional account gives you access to.

GroupChat — you can share a bot with classmates so a study group works off the same grounded tool instead of everyone re-explaining context to their own separate chat.

Verify before you trust

A study bot answering from your own course materials is still an AI system that can misstate, oversimplify, or overstate its confidence — grounding it in your materials reduces but doesn't eliminate that. Before you rely on anything it tells you for a grade, a claim in a paper, or a fact you didn't already know, check it against the source material or your instructor. Asking a second AI to check the first one isn't verification — go to a course source or a person.

What not to put in BoodleBox

Even on an institutionally licensed, privacy-protected platform, don't upload real client, patient, research-participant, or other identifiable sensitive data. Course materials, your own notes, and published readings are fine.

This guide describes BoodleBox's general features as of mid-2026; exact steps and menu names may shift as the platform updates. If something looks different on your screen, the underlying idea — ground it in your materials, give it a clear role, verify what it tells you — still applies.
